# Designing and reporting an informative experiment

Start with the decision this study could change. A useful result can justify stopping a line, expose a broken measurement or identify a worthwhile next test. Count its value against that decision. A reproducible number from the wrong comparison can still send the reader in the wrong direction.

## Before approval

Write a concrete plan for each experiment. The reviewer should be able to predict what would happen under at least two competing explanations and identify an observation that separates them.

| Question | Record in the plan |
|---|---|
| What would change our mind? | The target population, intervention, comparator, quantity to estimate and units. Choose the smallest effect that would change a decision. Give separate interpretations for a clear gain, a clear loss, a narrow interval near zero, an imprecise result and an invalid measurement. |
| What else predicts the same result? | Name plausible alternatives, including an already published effect, a measurement artifact and an ordinary explanation such as additional training. For each important rival, give its predicted pattern, a distinguishing comparison or control, its cost and the consequence if it remains unresolved. Count distinct predictions only when they help discriminate. |
| What does each condition actually receive? | Compare inputs, instructions, workload, opportunities, resources, feedback and scoring. Cross or counterbalance nuisance factors. In a framing study, holding the label constant while changing the task estimates a different contrast. Log explicit refusal, changed task, truncation, tool failure and completion separately, with an observable rule for each. |
| What can the instrument detect? | Include known positive and negative controls, range or ceiling checks and a known-answer fixture through the actual evaluator, device and data path. Specify a condition that would invalidate this assay and stop downstream work. |
| Have the baselines had a fair chance? | Match resources to the claim (equal compute, equal examples or comparable achieved fit). Prespecify checkpoint selection on validation data, a competence target and how to diagnose continuing progress. Inspect per-arm curves and relevant subgroups. Falling average training loss can coexist with a group that never learned. A short recovery after a schedule change may finish below the original endpoint. |
| What was kept independent? | Specify train, tuning/validation and final evaluation data, splitting by the unit that could leak (person, prompt family, source or time). Record overlap checks, preprocessing fitted on training data and earlier exposure to held-out outputs. Explain an alternative when a three-way split is inapplicable. Treat previously examined test data as development evidence for subsequent choices. |
| How much variation matters? | State the independent unit, nested repeats and paired or blocked design. Separate draws, questions, trained seeds and datasets. Specify how many independent units are feasible and what precision they buy for the important effect. Name which sources of variation the interval can cover. |
| How will evidence be analysed? | Freeze primary outcome and contrast, scale, direction, exclusions, missingness handling, stopping rule, and any sequential looks or multiplicity adjustment. Define which comparisons form a family. Separate prespecified secondary analyses from exploration. Compare conditions directly when claiming a difference between them. |
| When do we stop? | Name a cheap first check, maximum resource use and decision for each outcome. A problem that cannot be resolved in budget can justify stopping with the scientific question open. Show why doing another run would change a decision. |

Use a held-out unit or fresh data to confirm an explanation discovered in the results. Re-approval after seeing an outcome records a design change and what was already known. It cannot turn that outcome into a prospective test. The run directory preserves the approved plan and subsequent deviations. External preregistration requires submitting a plan to a registry. [Center for Open Science](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg)

Protect splits throughout selection and preprocessing. In comparisons between trained models, random initialisation, sampling, augmentation and hyperparameter selection can contribute different amounts of variation. Identify which are fixed by design and what generalisation that supports. [scikit-learn guidance on data leakage](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html) · [NeurIPS experimental reporting checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist) · [Bouthillier and colleagues on benchmark variance](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/0184b0cd3cfb185989f858a1d9f5c1eb-Abstract.html)

### Examples that change the design

- **Comparing optimisers.** At a short fixed training duration two methods appear separated on an evaluation curve. A second seed and a longer schedule bring them together. An approved plan should say whether it measures learning speed at a fixed budget or performance after adequate training. Before inferring the latter, specify how to check validation trajectories for each method and how to select comparable checkpoints. Retain unsuccessful and unmeasured cells.
- **Comparing task framings.** One arm invites refusal and the paired arm contains benign content. A lower completion rate is compatible with a content response even if the arithmetic reproduces exactly. Hold the underlying task, grader and available actions fixed across labels. Count stated refusals and task substitutions separately. Report a behavioral difference in these conditions. Inferring intent requires further evidence.
- **Selecting subgroups.** Sorting examples by measured baseline performance and then subtracting that same baseline from treatment performance can manufacture a gradient. Use an independent grouping variable, a different held-out measurement to assign bins, or report the pattern as descriptive.
- **Testing an instrument.** A model can answer the same nine questions correctly 151 times in 180 draws in one condition and only 54 times after reversing the options. Vary both orders before trusting the score. The 180 draws still represent nine distinct questions. A failed instrument check leaves the underlying trait unmeasured.

## During execution and audit

For each main claim, the experiment record links the approved contrast to the executed configuration, raw observation, baseline, estimate, uncertainty and evidence about its strongest rival. Write the time of any change, who had seen outcomes by then and the affected comparisons. Preserve the original verdict alongside a correction. An unrun arm is a missing comparison. A failed preflight may still produce descriptive measurements of the pipeline.

Recalculate the headline from primary records. Verify denominators and excluded, truncated or missing observations in each arm. For paired data estimate the paired difference. Respect clustering in uncertainty calculations and distinguish variability across examples from variability across independent training runs. Describe an interval using its level, method and resampling unit. One training seed cannot estimate variation across training seeds.

Report estimates in natural units (for example, percentage points), the comparison and an appropriate interval. High precision on an irrelevant metric does not make the finding important. A small p-value alone cannot establish practical importance. If an interval includes both meaningful gain and meaningful harm, report that uncertainty. To support practical equivalence, justify bounds tied to the decision in advance and use an appropriate equivalence procedure. Estimate interactions directly. Where several contrasts support a shared confirmatory claim, use an appropriate family-wise or false-discovery procedure, report the family and remaining exploratory comparisons. [American Statistical Association](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-ValueStatement.pdf) · [NIST on multiple comparisons](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section4/prc47.htm) · [Lakens on equivalence tests](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177)

Distinguish recalculating the same predictions, repeating a run with a fresh seed on the same stimuli, and testing on new held-out units. Each checks a different source of error. Record the actual scope and independence of reviewers, including any prior exposure to producer summaries. Resolve disagreements using the data or narrow the claim. When evidence is costly to obtain, retain the disagreement and explain how it affects the decision.

## The first screen Titus sees

1. Answer the motivating question in one sentence, within the scope the evidence supports. If it is unresolved, say why and give the most important observation that survived the audit.
2. Give the comparator, effect in familiar units, independent sample size and decision-relevant uncertainty. Put the strongest alternative or missing test next to the claim it qualifies.
3. Say what this changes for the research line. Recommend proceeding, stopping, or one discriminating next step with its cost. A method can be too expensive to pursue even while its scientific effect is unknown.
4. Put the headline figure or compact comparison immediately below. Link to the audit, the complete methods and denominators for readers who want to check them. Treat workflow completion, publication and scientific support as separate facts.

An example opening for a stopped image-classification study is “The filter lowered validation accuracy by 12.92 percentage points on one development seed. The planned three-seed confirmation never ran. We cannot estimate performance across training seeds.” Give the actual validation comparison in a plot or table and label the held-out test as unrun.

In prose, lead with observations people can check. Define unfamiliar measures, name the comparator in every directional claim and use consistent condition names throughout. Quantify a change before interpreting it. Put exploratory observations below the answer to the original question. Reserve causal and motivational language for designs that distinguish the proposed mechanism. Move background, workflow metadata and secondary results below the first screen.

## Figures and corrections

- Choose the display from the comparison. For a paired effect, show paired points or the within-pair differences. For dose or training trajectories, use comparable axes and show actual observations at their measured positions. For a single estimate with little variation to show, a sentence or small table can be enough.
- Show the baseline and treatment in meaningful units. Label direction, denominators, sample sizes and the quantity each interval represents. Where feasible show independent units or per-seed points, especially with few seeds. Avoid attaching uncertainty across a population of seeds to a single-seed pilot.
- Use shared axes for panels inviting direct comparison. Bars encode magnitude by length and need a meaningful zero. For lines or point estimates a restricted range may reveal an important change; label it clearly. Log scales need units and meaningful ticks. Display missing arms as gaps. Show uncertainty in the estimated contrast when that is the scientific claim.
- Generate figures from saved records and keep their inputs or generating code. Inspect each rendered headline figure at mail or paper width. Use direct series labels, distinguish series by marker or line style as well as colour, and check that the legend, title and captions agree with the data. Make the text alternative state the comparison and its direction. [Nature Communications statistical guidance](https://www.nature.com/documents/ncomms_-_statisticalguidance.pdf) · [UK Analysis Function accessible chart checklist](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/charts-a-checklist/)
- When correcting a result already shown to Titus, redraw the original figure with the corrected data. Preserve comparable axes, series and units. Identify which points changed, which are still missing and why the old reading changed. Update the title, briefing, abstract and next-step recommendation where affected. A reader who remembers the earlier chart should be able to see the correction on the same comparison.
